Teima, as in Sanhedrin 4b, where he said in his name that one who is blind in one of his eyes is exempt from the obligation of appearing [at the Temple on the pilgrimage festivals]. And the statement is brought in Ḥagigah 2a and Arakhin 2b, and there it says simply R' Yehuda, but in Dikdukei Soferim the reading is R' Yehuda b. Teima. And in Menaḥot 42b he disputes with R' Ḥanina b. Gamliel regarding tekhelet [the blue dye for fringes].

And an aggadic statement of his remains to us in Nedarim 20a, where he said: "The ministering angels told me four things: Why do lame children come to be, etc." And in Avot de-Rabbi Natan, at the end of chapter 27, he says: "One who says a halakhah is not clear (or: does not appear right) has no share in the World to Come." And he said there: "Do not distance yourself from a measure that has no limit and from a work that has no end," and he stated a fine parable there regarding this.
