This great Amora, although he did not merit to be the head of an academy, was nevertheless among the greatest disciples of Rav Huna and disseminated much Torah, and all the great Amoraim of the fourth generation were his disciples. Of his family and his father's house nothing is known to us, though as it appears, his father was not a Torah scholar, as is proven in Avodah Zarah 50b, where Rav Sheshet said: "I am neither a carpenter [scholar] nor the son of a carpenter, etc." About himself he spoke by way of humility, but how could he say so about his father? From this it is implied that his father was not a Torah scholar. His mother is mentioned in Bava Batra 9b, where Rav Sheshet became angry at Rav Aḥadvoi bar Ami and felt distressed; Rav Aḥadvoi bar Ami became mute and forgot his learning. His mother (of Rav Sheshet) came and wept before him. She cried out and cried out, and he paid no attention to her. She said to him: "Look at these breasts from which you nursed!" He prayed for mercy for him and he was healed. This is according to the commentary of Rashi; however, the opinion of Tosafot there is that the mother of Rav Aḥadvoi had been the wet-nurse of Rav Sheshet, and she came before him so that he would pray for her son. Rav Sheshet asked his attendant what Rav Ḥisda was doing, and he told him that he was standing and praying; he said to his attendant, "Set me up as well, that I may pray: 'If this wretch is pushed aside, before whom is he pushed aside?'" He said to him, "Leave him be." And at that time he was already blind, and therefore he called himself "a wretch." And likewise in Nedarim 78a: Rav Assi bar Natan found this Baraita difficult; he came to Nehardea before Rav Sheshet and did not find him; he came after him to Maḥoza and said to him, etc. And how he became blind is explained in the commentary of Rabbenu Gershom at the end of Menaḥot, and it is likewise brought in the Responsa of the Geonim, Shaarei Teshuvah, section 178: that Rav would not lift his eyes from the ground, and Rav Yosef and Rav Sheshet wished to conduct themselves thus, and they became blind. And likewise it is explicit in Berakhot 58a: Rav Sheshet was blind. Everyone was going to greet the face of the king, and Rav Sheshet arose and went with them. A certain Sadducee found him and said to him: "Whole pitchers go to the river; where do the broken shards go?" He said to him: "Come and see that I know more than you." ... And he was accustomed to be in the presence of the Exilarch, as in Berakhot 46b: when the Exilarch said to him, "Even though you are elderly rabbis, the Persians are more expert in the etiquette of a meal than you," he replied to him, "I know a baraita." And in Gittin 67b [printed 68a], the Exilarch asked him, "For what reason does the Master not dine with us?" He said to him, "Because your servants are suspected regarding a limb from a living animal." He said to him, "Who says so?" He said to him, "Now I will show you." He said to his attendant, "Go, steal and bring me one leg from the animal that was slaughtered today." He brought it to him. And afterward he commanded the servants to arrange all the pieces of the animal, and one leg was missing. He said to them, "Was this a three-legged animal?" They went and cut a leg from [a living] animal [and brought it]. And from this we see the greatness of his piety and his wisdom. And similarly we find that he used to inspect the slaughtering knife with the tip of his tongue, as in Ḥullin 17b; and he said, "May [reward] come to me, for I have fulfilled the commandment of tefillin," in Shabbat 118b. And his strictness always made an impression, as in Berakhot 58a with the Sadducee, and Bava Batra 9b with Rav Aḥadvoi bar Ami. And he did not merit male children, in Yevamot 62b: when the rabbis asked him for what reason he did not marry a woman to beget children, he replied to them, "My daughter's sons are to me like sons"; and the truth was that he had become sterile from the lecture of Rav Huna. And his passing is mentioned briefly in Moed Katan 28a, that once he (the Angel of Death) appeared to him in the marketplace. He said to him, "In the marketplace like an animal? Come to the house."
