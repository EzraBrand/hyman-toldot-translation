This one, distinguished among ten thousand, concerning whom it is said in Sukkah 20a: "At first, when Torah was forgotten from Israel, Ezra ascended from Babylonia and re-established it; when it was again forgotten, Hillel the Babylonian ascended and re-established it," was truly a wondrous man, a man sent from on high to save the fiery law from destruction, and upon whom Supreme Providence bestowed all its goodness, in lofty and exalted virtues and good traits, and in length of days.

הדגול מרבבה הלזה אשר עליו נאמר סוכה כ. בתחלה כשנשתכחה תורה מישראל עלה ﬠזרא מבבל ויסדה, חזרה ונשתכחה עלה הלל הבבלי ויסדה, היה באמת איש פלאי, איש אשר שולח ממרום להציל את אש-דת מכליון, ואשר ההשגחה העליונה האצילה ﬠליו את כל טובה, במﬠלות ומדות טובות רמות ונשגבות ובאריכת ימים.

Hillel is almost the only one among the Sages of blessed memory of whom it is known to us when he was born, how many years he was Nasi in Israel, and the days of his life, as is stated explicitly in Sifrei Berakhah end of 36 [perhaps 357], that Hillel lived 120 years: he went up from Babylonia at the age of forty years, attended the Sages for forty years, and led Israel for forty years. And in Shabbat 15a they said: "Hillel and Shimon [his son], Gamliel [the Elder] and Shimon [the martyr] exercised their patriarchate during the existence of the Temple for a hundred years" (meaning that these 4 Nesi'im served during the last hundred years before the Destruction). And since it is clearly known to us that Hillel was Nasi for forty years, if so, he died sixty years before the Destruction. And the Destruction of the Temple was in the year 3829–30, which is 382 of the Seleucid Era and seventy of the Common Era; if so, Hillel was born in the year 3650–1, 202 of the Seleucid Era.

Now, although it is impossible for us to deny for no reason that which is stated explicitly in the words of the Sages of blessed memory according to their true tradition—and in those generations it was no wonder to live one hundred and twenty years—nevertheless, the scholar R' Isaac Hirsch Weiss comes in Dor Dor ve-Dorshav, Part 1, page 147, in note 1, and after him Mr. Shlomo Berman in his book Mi-Giborei ha-Umah, and they state simply that Hillel lived eighty years, bringing no proof whatsoever for their words, only because such is their wish (and did not R' Isaac Hirsch Weiss himself live a long life of close to a hundred years?).

Now, if they will not believe the voice of the first sign, which is stated explicitly in the Sifrei, they will believe the voice of the latter sign, whereby against their will they must admit that Hillel lived no less than 120 years. For we find in Yalkut Ḥukkat, section 781 (from Sifrei Zuta, which is lost to us), and these are its words: "They asked Hillel in which garment the [Red] Heifer is burned. He said to them: 'In the high [priestly garments of gold].' They said to him: 'It is burned only in white [garments].' He said to them: 'I saw Yehoshua ben Peraḥyah burn it in the high [priestly garments].' They said to him: 'We saw that he burned it in white [garments].' — He said to them: 'Do not despise forgetfulness in a man, for if I forgot what my own eyes saw, etc.'" And so too it is brought in Sifrei, beginning of Ḥukkat, with the wording: "And some say it was Hillel the Elder." Now, we have already explained that Shemaiah and Avtalyon rose to greatness while Queen Shelominza [Salome Alexandra] was still reigning, because her brother Shimon ben Shetaḥ passed away before his sister, and at that time Hillel ministered as a disciple before them. And it is known that King Yannai reigned 27 years, and his brother reigned only one year, and a few years before Yoḥanan the High Priest joined the Sadducees; thus a period of 30 years passed from the days of the presidency of Yehoshua ben Peraḥyah, as we explained in his entry, when Yoḥanan the High Priest became a Sadducee and then Yehoshua ben Peraḥyah fled to Alexandria. And it is explicitly stated in Parah 3:5 that in the days of Yoḥanan the High Priest they prepared a Red Heifer, and without doubt it was prepared according to the ruling of the Nasi, R' Yehoshua ben Peraḥyah. From this, against our will, we must say that Hillel lived no less than 120 years. And without doubt, when they prepared the Red Heifer, it was done with great publicity until it was heard throughout all the dispersions of Israel; and the father of Hillel, who was without doubt among the great men of the generation in Babylonia, and perhaps was the Exilarch or from his family, as we shall explain, went up then with his young son Hillel to Jerusalem to see the great rejoicing that occurred in their days. At that time Hillel was truly very young in years, and perhaps had not yet reached his tenth year, and therefore he truly forgot what his eyes had seen. And I cannot believe that they would deny these true words, which were not said merely for the sake of a story, but rather regarding a matter of halakhah; and I think that this is more than enough to show the truth of the Sages, of blessed memory, that all their words are true.

Let us return to the biography of Hillel. He was a scion of the stock of the House of David who remained in Babylonia, as in Yerushalmi Ta'anit 4:2: R' Levi said, "A scroll of genealogy they found in Jerusalem, and in it was written: Hillel is from David." However, he was not from the male line of the House of David, as is explicit in Yerushalmi Kilayim 9:3 according to the testimony of R' Yehuda HaNasi his great-grandson and descendant, who said: "Rav Huna (the Exilarch) is from the males, and I am from the females," for he was from the tribe of Benjamin. And so it is in Bereishit Rabbah 98:8. However, in Ketubot 62b it is explicit that the family of R' Yehuda HaNasi was from Shefatyah son of Avital (the wife of David); and see Bereishit Rabbah 33[:3]; and in Tosafot Sanhedrin 5a, s.v. de-hakha.

The name of his father is not mentioned anywhere, and in Yerushalmi Eruvin 10:1 we find that Hillel the Elder said: "These tefillin are from the father of my mother." And we find that he had a brother by the name of Shevna, in Sotah 21a: When Rav Dimi came he said: "Hillel and Shevna were brothers. Hillel engaged in Torah (out of great poverty, Rashi), Shevna engaged in business. At the end he said to him: 'Come, let us mingle [our shares] and divide.' A heavenly voice went forth and said: 'If a man would give [all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned],' etc."

From whom he received the beginning of his study and what passed over him during all the first forty years of his life is not known to us at all, because we lack all the chronicles of the Babylonians from the time of the Temple. However, this is clearly known to us: that when he ascended to learn from the mouths of Shemayah and Avtalyon, he was then already the great man of the generation, as is explicit in Yerushalmi Pesaḥim, beginning of chapter 6, that "for three matters Hillel ascended from Babylonia" — and he expounded and agreed [with the halakhah], and ascended and received the halakhah. As is known, in the early generations there was no dispute in Israel, and all their study was only traditions from their teachers, and regarding any matter about which they had a doubt they sent or ascended to the Chamber of Hewn Stone, to the place of the Great Sanhedrin, from whom Torah went forth to all Israel. And so did Hillel do, for even though he expounded and arrived at the correct conclusion, nevertheless he was afraid to issue a practical halakhic ruling until he ascended to Shemayah and Avtalyon and received [tradition] from them that such was the halakhah.

And in Ha-Kerem 5648 [1888], page 164, the scholar Mr. Atlas says that in Hillel's youth no special talents for the study of the Torah were seen in him, and therefore when he ascended at about forty years of age from Babylonia to dedicate all his days to the Torah, Shevna his brother did not want to support him in this, because he considered him a man who would not succeed all his days. And all his words regarding the matters of Hillel are errors upon errors, as is explicit in the Yerushalmi that I brought, that when he came before Shemayah and Avtalyon he was already the great man of the generation, and not that he came to learn the alphabet from the mouths of the Nesi'im of Israel.

ובהכרם תרמ"ח צד 164 יאמר הרה"ח מר אטלס שבנﬠורי הלל לא נראו בו כשרונות מיוחדים ללמוד התורה ולכן כאשר ﬠלה כבן ארבﬠים שנה מבבל להקדיש כל ימיו לתורה לא רצה שבנא אחיו לתמכו בזה כי חשבו לגבר לא יצליח כל ימיו. וכל דבריו בדברי הלל המה שגיאות ﬠל שגיאות במפורש בירושל' שהבאתי שכשבא לפני שמﬠיה ואבטליון היה כבר גדול הדור, ולא שבא ללמוד מפי נשיאי ישראל את הא"ב.

And well spoke the rabbi, the author of Dorot ha-Rishonim, in Part 1, [page] 48, that there remains to us a remnant from the Torah of Hillel's teacher with whom he studied in Babylonia. It is brought in Eduyot 1:3, where they said: "Hillel says: A full hin of drawn water disqualifies the mikveh; only, a person is obligated to state [a teaching] in the language of his teacher — until two weavers came — and testified in the name of Shemayah and Avtalyon that three log of drawn water disqualify." And the commentators on this are many. Rashi explains in Shabbat 15a that "hin" is not the language of the Mishnah, but the language of the Torah; however, according to his explanation, it will be difficult regarding Shemayah and Avtalyon themselves: how did they change the language of the Mishnah? And the Rambam explains [that it was] because they were converts and their speech remained the speech of gentiles, and they would say "in" instead of "hin." And according to his explanation it is difficult: a) for they were only from a family of converts; b) if one's teacher has a stammering tongue, is his student also obligated to stutter with his tongue? Is this a mitzvah?

And the Vilna Gaon says a nice thing: that the word "full" [melo] is superfluous. For it is well that they [Shemayah and Avtalyon] could not say "hin" and therefore had to say "a full hin," because if they had not said "full," it would have sounded like "drawn water does not [ein] disqualify"; however, Hillel, who was able to pronounce "hin"—if so, why did he need to say the word "full"? And regarding this they said: "Only, a person is obligated to state [a teaching] in the language of his teacher." However, it is a wonder that it happened that both of them alike could not pronounce the letter heh. Furthermore, in the Mishnah itself they said that the weavers testified in their name that only three log disqualify, and not like the words of Hillel who said in their name that a full hin disqualifies; and where is his faithful tradition, seeing that he served Shemayah and Avtalyon all his days?

והגר"א אומר דבר נחמד שתיבת מלא היא מיותר, דבשלמא המה לא יכלו לומר "הין" ולכן הוצרכו לומר "מלא הין", שאם לא אמרו "מלא" היה משמע "אין מים שאובין פוסלין", אבל הלל שהיה יכול לדבר "הין" א"כ למה הוצרך לומר תיבת "מלא"? וע"ז אמרו אלא שחייב אדם לומר בלשון רבו. אבל לפלא שאירע ששניהם כאחד לא יכלו לדבר הה"א, ועוד הלא במשנה עצמה אמרו שהעידו הגרדיים משמם שאך ג' לוגין פוסלין ולא כדברי הלל שאמר משמם שמלא הין פוסלין, ואיה קבלתו הנאמנה ששימש כל ימיו את שמעיה ואבטליון.

Therefore, the aforementioned scholar [the author of Dorot ha-Rishonim] said that his teacher who studied with Hillel in Babylonia, when he taught him the Mishnah, was forced to use the language of the Torah, because the measures were constantly changing from the wilderness [measures] to the Jerusalemite, and afterward to the Sepphorean, and in Babylonia there was without doubt a different measure; therefore, when the Babylonians studied the Mishnah concerning the measures, they needed to study in the language of the Torah, "hin," whereby it is impossible to err. And Hillel, even when he came to Eretz Yisrael, did not change from the language of his Babylonian teacher, whereas Shemayah and Avtalyon had received a different measure. And similar to this I found in the Arukh, entry "Lashon": "'For a person is obligated to state [a teaching] in the language of his teacher' — meaning, he did not state the measure of log, but 'a full hin of water,' just as the Torah measures by the hin, and 'his teacher' means Moses our Teacher." The Time of Hillel's Coming a Second Time to Jerusalem and His Appointment as Nasi.

זמן ביאת הלל פﬠם שנית לירושלים והתמנותו לנשיא.

If we wish to look with an open eye, there will appear to our eyes a wondrous thing and divine providence, that the cure was created even before the blow. For when the rod of His anger was sent from on high—this Edomite slave, who almost made an end of the people of Israel, and for whom there was no end to the tens of thousands of Israel whom he made ary [?] for the trampling of his feet and the feet of his servants, members of a foreign people, and who was very far from the light of the religion of Israel—He prepared before this the man distinguished among ten thousand, Hillel the Babylonian. And the Lord repaid him measure for measure: in return for having endangered himself and climbed up to the opening of the skylight to hear the words of the living God from the mouths of Shemayah and Avtalyon, he was therefore exalted to illuminate the eyes of the entire House of Israel; and in return for having fulfilled in himself (Mishnah, Avot 6:4): "This is the way of Torah—and you shall live a life of hardship and toil in the Torah," He brought him to the high and exalted degree of placing the crown of the Nesi'ut upon his head. Concerning him we can say the verse: "Now there was found in it a poor wise man, and he by his wisdom delivered the city" (Ecclesiastes 9:15). He, in the company of his great colleague Shammai and their disciples, together rebuilt the ruined House of Israel; they built a strong building, an everlasting building, a spiritual building which neither Herod, nor the kings of Rome, nor the kings of the rest of the nations of the world could approach within to destroy; and they were able to rule only over their bodies, but not over their exalted spirit and the perfect Torah of the Lord that restores the soul.

And in Josephus, in Antiquities Book 15, 10:1, he says that when Herod captured Jerusalem and carried out a great slaughter in Israel, he greatly honored the two Pharisees, who are Pollio and Sameas, because during the days of the siege, when he besieged Jerusalem, those two Pharisees advised opening the gates of the city before him. Who are the two Pharisees whom Josephus mentions by the name Pollio and Sameas? And the name Sameas he mentions in Antiquities Book 14, 9:4, when Herod came before the Sanhedrin and all the sages of the Sanhedrin were afraid of him, then Sameas arose and spoke without awe or fear. And together they are mentioned twice: a) in Antiquities Book 15, 1:1, regarding the matter that they advised opening the gates of the city before Herod, as we cited above; and further there, when he wanted to make the people swear that they would be loyal to him, then Pollio and Sameas did not want to swear, and he did them no harm.

והנה כבר הבאנו דﬠת חז"ל הנאמנה שהורדוס מלך ג' שנים טרם נתמנה הלל לנשיא. וביוזיפוס בקדמוניות ספר טו פ"י-א יאמר כשלכד הורדוס את ירושלים וﬠשה הרג רב בישראל כיבד מאוד את שני הפרושים המה Pollio and Sameas יﬠן כי בימי המצור כשצר ﬠל ירושלים יﬠצו שני הפרושים ההם לפתוח את שﬠרי הﬠיר לפניו, מי המה שני הפרושים אשר יזכיר יזיפוס בשם Pollio and Sameas ושם Sameas יזכיר בקדמוניות ספר י"ד פ"ט-ד כשבא הורדוס לפני הסנהדרין וכל חכמי הסנהדרין פחדו מפניו אז קם Sameas ודיבר בלי מורא ופחד, וביחד נזכרו שתי פﬠמים א) בקדמוניות ספר טו פ"א-א בדבר שהמה יﬠצו לפתוח שﬠרי הﬠיר לפני הורדוס כמו שהבאנו למﬠלה. וﬠוד שם כשרצה להשביﬠ את הﬠם שיהיו נאמנים לו אז Pollio and Sameas לא רצו לישבﬠ ולא ﬠשה להם רﬠה.

Now, they all prophesy in one style that Josephus intended Shemaiah and Avtalyon, such as Rabbi Zechariah Frankel in Darkhei HaMishnah page 37, and likewise the scholar Derenbourg in his precious book Masa Eretz Yisrael page 39, that the name Pollio and Sameas mentioned in Yosippon [Josephus] in Antiquities, when Herod killed the sages approximately ten years after becoming king and spared for good Pollio and Sameas, they are Shemaiah and Avtalyon. And the gatherer of all the camps [comprehensive compiler], Rabbi Isaac Hirsch Weiss, in Dor Dor ve-Dorshav Part 1 page 143 says: "When Herod came to the throne, his first act was to pursue the Sanhedrin in anger—and against Shemaiah and Avtalyon he did not stretch out his hand, for he did not forget that at the time he fought with Antigonus and besieged Jerusalem they were advising the people to surrender the city to him, and Shemaiah he honored with awe, remembering the words that he spoke on the day he stood before the Sanhedrin for judgment, when he foretold the events to come, that Herod would be the rod, etc., Antiquities Book 15:1."

והנה כולם מתנבאים בסגנון אחד שכונה יוזיפוס ﬠל שמﬠיה ואבטליון כמו הרב רז"פ בדרכי המשנה צד 37, וכן החכם דﬠרנבורג בספרו היקר משא א"י צד לט. ששם פוליון וסﬠמיוס הנזכר ביוסיפון בקדמוניות כשהרג הורדוס את החכמים לﬠרך ﬠשר שנים אחרי מלכו והשאיר לטובה את Pollio and Sameas המה שמﬠיה ואבטליון והמאסף לכל המחנות הראה"ו בדו"ד ח"א צד 143 יאמר: "כאשר בא הורדוס למלוכה היתה ראשית מﬠשהו לרדוף באף את הסנהדרין - וכשמﬠיה ואבטליון לא שלח ידו כי לא שכח שבﬠת נלחם ﬠם אנטיגנוס ויצר ﬠל ירושלים היו יוﬠצים לﬠם למסור לו את העיר ואת שמﬠיה כבד במורא בזכרו את הדברים אשר דבר ביום ﬠמדו לפני הסנהדרין לדין אשר הגיד האותיות לאחור כי הורדוס יהיה השבט וכו' קדמוניוח סט"ו־א.

And with all due respect, they all erred in all three places, and not only in the last two places when they advised opening the gates of Jerusalem before Herod and in the matter of the oath, where it is beyond all doubt that his intention is only to Hillel and Shammai, for there Josephus says explicitly "Pollio and Sameas his disciple" (Pollio and Sameas his disciple), and in such a matter it is impossible for Josephus to err, for it was known to all that Shemaiah was the Nasi and Avtalyon the second to him, and if so, how could Josephus call Shemaiah a disciple? Rather, it is beyond all doubt that his intention is to Hillel and Shammai, and he called Shammai a disciple because he was the second to Hillel, and all are subordinate under the Nasi to be called by the name of disciple.

ובמחילה כולם טﬠו בכל השלשה מקומות ולא לבד בשני מקומות האחרונים כשיﬠצו לפתוח שﬠרי ירושלים לפני הורדוס ובדבר השבוﬠה ששם נﬠלה מﬠל כל ספק שכונתו אך ﬠל הלל ושמאי ששם יאמר יוזיפוס בפירוש Pollio and Sameas his disciple (פאלליא וסאמיס תלמידו) ובדבר כזה אי אפשר לו ליוזיפוס לטﬠות כי היה ידוﬠ לכל ששמﬠיה היה הנשיא ואבטליון השני לו, ואם כן איך קרא יוזיפוס את שמﬠיה לתלמיד? אבל נﬠלה מﬠל כל ספק שכונתו ﬠל הלל ושמאי וקרא את שמאי תלמיד יﬠן שהוא היה השני להלל וכולם כפופים תחת הנשיא להקרא בשם תלמיד.

And regarding what he wrote that Hillel and Shammai advised opening the gates of Jerusalem before Herod, the truth is according to his words, and it does not contradict the words of our Sages of blessed memory in what they said in Shabbat 15a, that the reign of Herod began three years before Hillel ascended to the office of Nasi, for the words of both of them are true, because our Sages of blessed memory had a true tradition as to when Herod was set up as king, and their intention was from the day he was made king at the hands of the Caesar in Rome, because a king over Israel he never was, for he ruled and governed by force of hand, and they never accepted the Idumean as king over them willingly.

ומה שכתב שהלל ושמאי יﬠצו לפתוח שﬠרי ירושלים לפני הורדוס האמת הוא כדבריו ואינו סותר דברי חז"ל מה שאמרו שבת טו. שמלכות הורדוס התחילה שלש שנים טרם ﬠלה הלל לנשיאות, כי דברי שניהם אמת יﬠן כי לחז"ל היה להם קבלה אמיתית מתי הﬠמד הורדוס למלך וכונתן מיום שנﬠשה מלך מידי הקיסר ברומי, יﬠן כי מלך ﬠל ישראל לא היה מﬠולם כי רדה ומשל בחזקת היד, ומﬠולם לא קבלו את האדומי למלך עליהם בנפש חפצה.

Now, from the day he was established as king until he conquered Jerusalem was a period of time close to four years, as is brought in Josephus, and within these four years Hillel came to Jerusalem, or it is possible that Hillel had already been in Jerusalem sitting as a private individual, and the incident of the Bnei Bathyra occurred (and as we shall bring further on) and he was established as Nasi. And therefore Josephus was right in saying that by the advice of Pollio and Sameas the gates were opened before Herod, and therefore Dorot HaRishonim Part 1 page 42 was not right in saying that Josephus erred in this.

והנה מיום שהוקם למלך ﬠד שכבש את ירושלים היתה משך זמן קרוב לארבﬠ שנים כדמובא ביוזיפוס, ובתוך הד' שנים הללו בא הלל לירושלים או אפשר שהלל היה בירושלים מכבר יושב כאיש פרטי, ואירﬠ המﬠשה דבני בתירא (ובאשר נביא הלאה) והוקם לנשיא, ולכן צדק היוזיפוס באמרו שﬠל פי ﬠצת Pollio and Sameas נפתחו השﬠרים לפני הורדוס, ולכן לא צדק דוה"ר ח"א צד 42 שיאמר שיאזﬠפוס טﬠה בזה.

Moreover, even what he brought in Antiquities, that when Herod stood before the seat of judgment before the Sanhedrin then Sameas arose, his intention is also исключительно to Shammai and not to Shemaiah, for Shemaiah and Avtalyon had already passed away, and at that time the elders of Bnei Bathyra were in the place of the Nesi'im, yet they did not arrogate authority to themselves to sit at the head, and especially since at that time the Sanhedrin had been divided into five places, and Shammai was then only one of the members of the Sanhedrin. For if his intention were to Shemaiah, if so, why did he get angry at his colleagues because they were silent and did not speak anything against Herod? Was he not the Nasi, and upon whom was the obligation to issue a righteous judgment if not upon the Nasi alone?

אבל גם מה שהביא בקדמוניות כשﬠמד הורדוס לפני כסא המשפט לפני הסנהדרין אז קם Sameas ג"כ כונתו אך ורק ﬠל שמאי ולא ﬠל שמﬠיה כי שמﬠיה ואבטליון כבר נפטרו ואז היו זקני בני בתירא במקום הנשיאים אך לא לקחו להם קרנים לישב בראש ובפרט שאז נתחלקו הסנהדרין לחמש מקומות, ושמאי היה אז אך אחד מחברי הסנהדרין, שאם כונתו ﬠל שמﬠיה א"כ למה קצף על חביריו מה ששתקו ולא דברו מאומת נגד הורדוס? הלא הוא הנשיא וﬠל מי החובה היה להוציא משפט צדק אם לא ﬠל הנשיא לבדו.

Rather, it is as we have said, that the speaker was only Shammai and not Shemaiah, and when he saw that the heads of the Sanhedrin put their hand to their mouth and were afraid before Herod (even though justice was with them, for what could they do when before their eyes stood armed men with the flash of their swords)—and this is the wording of the English translation in Antiquities Book 14, 9:4:

אבל זה אשר אמרנו שהמדבר היה אך שמאי ולא שמﬠיה וכאשר ראה שראשי הסנהדרין ישימו יד לפה ומפחדין מפני הורדוס (אע"פ שהצדק היה אתם כי מה יוכלו לﬠשות אם נגד ﬠיניהם נצבו אנשי חיל בלהט חרבותם) וזה לשון הﬠתקת האנגלי באנטיקווﬠטי ספר יד' פ"ט־ד:

And if Shemaiah had been the Nasi then, without any doubt he would have written that the Nasi or the head of the Sanhedrin arose and spoke. And in the book Bikoret HaTalmud page 282 he copies that Shammai and Hillel were then[?] among the Sanhedrin, and he testified falsely.

ואם היה אז שמﬠיה הנשיא בלי שום ספק היה כותב שקם הנשיא או ראש הסנהדרין ודיבר. ובספר בקורת התלמוד צד 282 יﬠתיק ששמאי והלל היו או בין הסנהדרין ושקר הﬠיד.

And this is what Josephus said, that Herod honored these two Pharisees because they advised opening the gates of Jerusalem before him, and Sameas he honored further because he remembered what he had spoken about him when he stood before the Sanhedrin. And in truth, when Herod stood before the Sanhedrin for judgment, Hillel was not there then, for this was many years before Hillel came to Jerusalem. And let us return to our subject.

וזה שאמר יוזיפוס שכיבד הורדוס את שני הפרושים האלו יﬠן שיﬠצו לפתוח שﬠרי ירושלים לפניו ואת Sameas כיבד עוד יען שזכר מה שדיבר עליו בעמדו לפני הסנהדרין, ובאמת כשעמד הורדוס לפני הסנהדרין לדין לא היה אז הלל שמה, כי זה היה שנים הרבה טרם בא הלל לירושלים, ונחזור לעניינו.

When Hillel came to Jerusalem for the second time, he found the great destruction of the people and the Torah. Herod waged war with the sword of his arrogance against Israel, his teachers had already passed away and others had not been raised up in their stead, for although Antigonus the son of Aristobulus reigned for a few years, the wrath of Herod immediately leaped upon him; therefore, when he came to Jerusalem, he sat silent and alone in his tent and engaged in Torah. And when his time came to be elevated as Nasi, it is recounted in Pesaḥim 66a and in Yerushalmi there at the start of chapter 6, that one time the fourteenth [of Nisan] (the eve of Passover) fell on Shabbat. They (the Bnei Bathyra) forgot and did not know whether the Passover offering overrides Shabbat or not (and if the years had been in their proper order, then the law would have been decided by the Sanhedrin and the Nasi). They said: "There is a certain Babylonian here, and Hillel is his name, who served Shemaiah and Avtalyon; he knows whether the Passover offering overrides Shabbat or not." They sent and called for him, and when he came before them, he began to expound for them from analogy [hekeish], and from a fortiori [kal va-ḥomer], and from verbal analogy [gezeirah shavah] (and so it is explicit in Avot de-Rabbi Natan chapter 37 that he expounded for them all the seven hermeneutical rules), yet they did not accept it from him and rejected all his proofs, and they said to him: "We have already said, is there any hope from a Babylonian?" (and from this is an absolute proof that the Bnei Bathyra were not Babylonians). And even though he sat and expounded all day, they did not accept it from him, until he said to them: "So may it befall me, thus I heard from Shemaiah and Avtalyon." As soon as they heard thus from him, they arose and appointed him Nasi over them.

כשבא הלל לירושלים בפעם השנית ומצא את גודל חורבן העם והתורה. הורדוס עשה מלחמה בחרב גאותו על ישראל, רבותיו כבר נפטרו ואחרים לא הוקמו תחתיהם כי אף שמלך אנטיננוס בן אריסטובלוס שנים אחדות אבל תיכף קפצה עליו רוגזת הורדוס, לכן כשבא לירושלים ישב דומם בדד באהלו ועסק בתורה, וכשהגיע שעתו להתנשא, יסופר בפסחים סו. ובירושלמי שם רפ"ו שפ"א חל יד' (ערב פסח) להיות בשבת שכחו (בני בתירא) ולא ידעו אם פסח דוחה את השבת אם לאו (ואם היו השנים כתקונם אז היה נחתך הדין על פי הסנהדרין והנשיא), אמרו יש כאן בבלי אחד והלל שמו ששימש את שמעיה ואבטליון יודע אם פסח דוחה את השבת אם לא. שלחו וקראו לו, וכשבא לפניהם התחיל לדרוש להם מהיקש ומקל וחומר ומגזירה שווה, (וכן מפורש באבות דרבי נתן פל"ז שדרש להם כל הז' מדות) אך הם לא קבלו ממנו ודחו את כל ראיותיו ואמרו לו כבר אמרנו אם יש תוחלת מבבלי (ומזה ראיה גמורה שבני בתירא לא היו בבליים), ואע"פ שהיה יושב ודורש כל היום לא קבלו ממנו, עד שאמר להם יבוא עלי כך שמעתי משמעיה ואבטליון, כיון ששמעו ממנו כן עמדו ומינו אותו נשיא עליהן.

And this is because for a great matter like this, one needs only a simple tradition from the mouth of one's teachers, and therefore as long as he wanted to show them the law by the power of dialectical reasoning [pilpul], they showed him that they were not inferior to him; however, in the end, when he told them that thus he had received from his teachers, then they saw that they had found a man of exalted stature who held all the tradition of the Torah in his hand, and he was the very man whom they were seeking to heal the breaches. And in Tosefta Pesaḥim at the start of chapter 4, they said that all this matter happened and took place in the Temple Courtyard, and this is a sign that the matter was not done publicly in the Chamber of Hewn Stone, the seat of the Sanhedrin, because in truth he was not elected Nasi in the manner that his teachers were elected, for the power of the Sanhedrin had already been taken away from the day that Shemaiah and Avtalyon passed away, and there remained only houses for Torah; and when they found that which they sought, then they appointed him Nasi over them, that is, according to the power that they had in their presidency, and they trusted in him that he would gradually restore the power of the Sanhedrin as at first.

וזה יען כי לדבר גדול כזה צריכין אך קבלה פשוטה מפי רבותיו, ולכן כ"ז שרצה להראות להם הדין בכח הפלפול הראו לו כי לא נופלים הם ממנו, אבל לבסוף שאמר להם כי כן קיבל מרבותיו אז ראו כי מצאו איש רם המעלה שכל קבלת התורה בידו. והוא הוא האיש אשר המה מבקשים לרפא הנשברות. ובתוספתא פסחים רפ"ד אמרו שכל הדבר הזה נקרה ואתא בעזרה, וזה אות כי לא נעשה הדבר בפומבי בלשכת הגזית מקום מושב הסנהדרין יען כי באמת לא נבחר לנשיא כדרך שנבחרו רבותיו כי כבר נוטל כח הסנהדרין מיום שנפטרו שמעיה ואבטליון ואך נשארו בתים לתורה, וכשמצאו את אשר בקשו אז מינוהו לנשיא עליהם היינו כפי מה שהיה להם הכח בנשיאותם ובטחו בו כי הוא יקים מעט מעט את כח הסנהדרין כבראשונה.

And it is recounted there that he began to chide them with words, and he said to them: "Who caused you to have need of this Babylonian? Was it not because you did not serve the two great men of the world?" Because he chided them, a halakhah was concealed from him; for they asked him and said to him: "Master, if one forgot and did not bring a knife on the eve of Shabbat, what is the law?" He said to them: "This halakhah I heard and I have forgotten; rather, leave it to Israel: if they are not prophets, they are the sons of prophets" (and they themselves will act according to the law), and so it was.

ויסופר שם שהתחיל לקנטרן בדברים וא"ל מי גרם לכם לצרך להבבלי הזה לא על שלא שימשתם לשני גדולי עולם - כיון שקינטרן נתעלמה הלכה ממנו כי שאלוהו וא"ל רבי, שכח ולא הביא סכין מע"ש מהו? א"ל הלכה זו שמעתי ושכחתי אלא הניחו לישראל אם אינם נביאים הן, בני נביאים הן (והמה בעצמם יעשו כדין) וכן היה.

And Rabbi Isaac Hirsch Weiss in Part 1 page 144 pounces upon his prey and says: "For in the days of Shemaiah and Avtalyon the halakhot were fruitful and multiplied—and from this it begot and sprouted a division of opinions even among the Pharisees—and after their death two men stood at the head of the Sanhedrin, they are the Bnei Bathyra, who were not among those who frequented their study hall—and when they forgot whether the Passover offering overrides Shabbat, and Hillel came and expounded for them with the hermeneutical rules by which the Torah is expounded, they did not accept it from him; from this we see that they did not want exegesis based on the hermeneutical rules," thus far his words. And it is a wonder that he does not hear for himself what he is saying! For if so, why did they remove the office of Nasi from upon themselves willingly and hand it over to the man most opposed to them, whose Torah was only from exegesis based on the hermeneutical rules?

והראה"ו בח"א צד 144 יתנפל על טרפו ויאמר "כי בימי שמעיה ואבטליון פרו ורבו ההלכות - ומזה הולידה והצמיחה פירוד הדעות גם בין הפרושים- ואחרי מותם עמדו שני אנשים בראש הסנהדרין הם בני בתירא אשר לא היו מבאי בית מדרשם - וכששכחו אם פסח דוחה השבת ובא הלל ודרש להם במדות שהתורה נדרשה לא קבלו ממנו, מזה אנו רואים כי הם לא היו רוצים במדרש ע"פ המדות, עכ"ל." ולפלא שלא ישמע בעצמו מה שהוא מדבר. שא"כ למה הסירו מעליהם הנשיאות בנפש חפצה ומסרו לאיש היותר מתנגד להם אשר תורתו אך ממדרש ע"פ המדות?

And so he says further on page 145: "For it is very wondrous how men not distinguished in wisdom reached the highest rank in the nation—however, let us cast an eye upon [someone] walking before him; once he did not find a servant, and he ran before him three mils." [? — text appears truncated in the original, merging Weiss's quote with Ketubot 67b]. And in Beitzah 20a: "An incident involving Hillel the Elder, who brought his burnt-offering to the Temple courtyard to lay hands upon it on a festival day (in accordance with his view that one lays hands on a festival day). The disciples of Shammai the Elder gathered against him and said to him: 'What is the nature of this animal?' He said to them: 'It is a female, and I brought it as a peace-offering'" (out of his great humility he altered [the truth] for the sake of peace, Rashi). And in Shabbat 17a: "That day Hillel sat bent and submissive before Shammai like one of the disciples, and it was as grievous to Israel [as the day the golden calf was made]" (because Hillel was the Nasi and humble, Rashi).

וכן יאמר הלאה בצד 145 "כי יפלא מאד הדבר איך הגיעו אנשים בלתי מצויינים בחכמה למעלה היותר גבוה באומה - אמנם נשים עין על מהלך לפניו פ"א לא מצא עבד, ורץ לפניו שלשה מילים. וביצה כ. מעשה בהלל הזקן שהביא עולתו לעזרה לסמוך עליה ביו"ט (כשיטתו שסומכין ביו"ט) חברו עליו תלמידי שמאי הזקן אמרו לו מה טיבה של בהמה זו? א"ל נקבה היא ולזבחי שלמים הבאתיה (מרוב ענותנותיה היה משנה מפני השלום, רש"י). ובשבת יז. אותו היום היה הלל כפוף ויושב לפני שמאי כאחד מן התלמידים וההי קשה לישראל (לפי שהלל היה נשיא וענוותן, רש"י).

And from everything that happened to him he took a moral lesson, for the fact that he had taunted the Bnei Bathyra with words and as a result the halakhah was concealed from him served as a beneficial teacher to him, so that he never became angry all his days. And as is recounted in Shabbat 30b: Our Rabbis taught: A person should always be humble like Hillel. There was an incident involving two men who made a wager with each other, saying that whoever could go and provoke Hillel would take four hundred zuz. That day was Sabbath eve, and Hillel was washing his head. One of them went and passed by the door of his house and said: "Who here is Hillel? Who here is Hillel?" Hillel wrapped himself and went out to meet him, and said to him: "My son, what do you seek?" He said to him: "I have a question to ask—why are the heads of the Babylonians oval?" He said to him: "My son, you have asked a great question: because they do not have skillful midwives" (and it is possible that Hillel's head was oval, and he wanted to anger him with this). He went and waited one hour, returned, and said: "Who here is Hillel?" And so he did several times, and at the end he said to him: "I have many questions to ask, and I am afraid lest you become angry." Hillel wrapped himself and sat before him and said to him: "Ask all the questions you have to ask." He said to him: "Are you the Hillel whom they call the Nasi of Israel?" He said to him: "Yes." He said to him: "If you are he, may there not be many like you in Israel." He said to him: "My son, because of what?" He said to him: "Because I have lost four hundred zuz through you." He said to him: "Be careful of your spirit; Hillel is worthy that you should lose four hundred zuz and yet another four hundred zuz through him, and Hillel will not lose his temper." And it is recounted there [Shabbat 31a] that a certain gentile came before Shammai on condition that he teach him only the Written Torah, because he did not believe in the Oral Torah, and he rebuked him and drove him out with a reprimand. When he came before Hillel, he converted him. On the first day he taught him Alef, Bet, Gimel, Dalet; the next day he reversed them for him. He said to him: "But yesterday you did not tell me thus!" He said to him: "Did you not rely on me? Then regarding the Oral [Torah] also, rely on me!" And another gentile came before Shammai on condition that he teach him the entire Torah while he stood on one foot; he pushed him away with the builder's cubit that was in his hand. And Hillel converted him, and said to him: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow: this is the entire Torah, and the rest is its commentary; go and learn." And yet another gentile came before Shammai to be converted on condition that they appoint him High Priest, and he pushed him away. And Hillel converted him, and when he studied the Torah and learned that even regarding an Israelite it is written, "and the common man who draws near shall be put to death," he drew an a fortiori conclusion regarding himself. When he came before Hillel, he said to him: "O humble Hillel, may blessings rest upon your head!" In time, the three of them happened to meet in one place; they said: "Shammai's impatience sought to drive us from the world, [whereas] Hillel's humility brought us under the wings of the Divine Presence." And they said in Avot de-Rabbi Natan at the end of chapter 15 that to the last one two sons were born; one he named Hillel and one Gamaliel, and they used to call them "Hillel's proselytes." And in Tosefta Berakhot at the end of chapter 2, he said: "Do not appear naked [when others are clothed], and do not appear clothed [when others are naked], etc." (and his intention was that a person's mind should be sociable with people and he should not deviate [from the norm]).

ומכל דבר שאירע לו לקח מוסר כי מה שקינטר את בני בתירא בדברים ונתעלמה ממנו ההלכה היה לו למלמד להועיל שלא כעס כל ימיו. וכאשר יסופר שבת ל: ת"ר לעולם יהא אדם ענוותן כהלל, ומעשה בשני ב"א שהמרו שכל מי שיכול להקניט את הלל יטול ארבע מאות זוז, ואותו היום ע"ש היה הלך אחד מהן ועבר על פתח ביתו בשעה שחפף את ראשו ואמר מי כאן הלל מי כאן הלל. נתעטף הלל ויצא לקראתו וא"ל בני מה אתה מבקש, א"ל שאלה יש לי לשאול - מ"מ ראשיהן של בבליים סגלגלות, א"ל בני שאלה גדולה שאלת מפני שאין להם חיות פקחות (ואפשר שראש הלל היה סגלגל ורצה להכעיסו בזה), הלך והמתין שעה אחת חזר ואמר מי כאן הלל, וכן עשה כמה פעמים ובאחרונה א"ל שאלות הרבה יש לי לשאול ומתיירא אני שמא תכעוס, נתעטף וישב לפניו וא"ל שאל ממני כל שאלותיך, א"ל אתה הוא הלל שקורין אותך נשיא ישראל? א"ל הן א"ל אם אתה הוא לא ירבו כמותך בישראל. א"ל בני מפני מה? א"ל מפני שאבדתי ע"י ד' מאות זוז, א"ל הוי זהיר ברוחך כדי הוא הלל שתאבד על ידו ד' מאות זוז וד' מאות זוז והלל לא יקפיד. ויסופר שם שעכו"ם אחד בא לפני שמאי ע"מ שילמדנו אך תורה שבכתב כי לא האמין בתורה שבע"פ וגער בו והוציאו בנזיפה וכשבא לפני הלל גייריה, ביום הראשון למדו אבג"ד, למחר אפיך ליה א"ל והא אתמול לא אמרת לי הכי, א"ל לאו עלי דידי קא סמכת דע"פ נמי סמוך עלי. ועוד עכו"ם אחד בא לפני שמאי ע"מ שילמדהו כל התורה כשהוא עומד על רגל אחת דחפו באמת הבנין והלל גייריה וא"ל דעלך סני לחברך לא תעביד זו היא כל התורה כולה ואידך פירושה הוא זיל גמור. ועוד עכו"ם אחד בא לפני שמאי שיתגייר ע"מ שישימוהו כה"ג ודחפו. והלל גיירו וכשלמד התורה וידע שגם בישראל כתיב והזר הקרוב יומת נשא קו"ח בעצמו. וכשבא לפני הלל א"ל ענוותן הלל ינוחו לך ברכות על ראשך - לימים נזדמנו שלשתן למקום אחד אמרו ענוותנותו של הלל קרבנו תחת כנפי השכינה, ואמרו באבות דר"נ ספט"ו שלהאחרון נולד שני בנים לאחד קרא הלל ולאחד גמליאל והיו קוראין אותם גרים של הלל. ובתוספתא ברכות ספ"ב אמר אל תראה ערום ואל תראה לבוש וכו', (וכונתו שיהא דעתו של אדם מעורב עם הבריות ולא ישנה).

His trust in the Lord was wondrous, and he said, "Blessed be the Lord day by day," as in Beitzah 16a. And once when he was coming from the road he heard the sound of an outcry in the city; he said: "I am confident that this is not within my house," as in Berakhot 60a. And he said that a person must strive always without cease to ascend higher and higher in his spiritual strength, and not do anything for the sake of his imaginary honor. Among his precious sayings in Avot 1:13 is that he said: "A name made great is a name destroyed; he who does not increase [his knowledge] decreases [it]; and he who does not study deserves death." And he said [Avot 1:14]: "If I am not for myself, who will be for me? And when I am for myself alone, what am I?" And he said: "My abasement is my exaltation; my exaltation is my abasement," Vayikra Rabbah 1:5. And his precious saying in Sukkah 53a, when he was rejoicing at the Rejoicing of the Place of the Water-Drawing (and was exalted in the awe of [God's] majesty), he said: "If I am here, all is here; and if I am not here, who is here?" (for the good and the evil that a person does, he does it all for himself).

בטחונו בה' היה עד להפליא ואמר ברוך ה' יום יום כביצה טז. ופ"א כשבא מן הדרך שמע קול צוחה בעיר אמר מובטחני שאין זה בתוך ביתי כברכות ס. ואמר שצריך האדם להשתדל תמיד בלי הרך לעלות מעלה מעלה בכוחו הרוחני ולא לעשות שום דבר בשביל כבדו המדומה. ממאמריו היקרים אבות פ"א מ"יג שאמר נגיד שמיה אבד שמו ודלא מוסיף יסוף ודלא יליף קטלא חייב. ואמר אם אין אני לי מי לי וכשאני לעצמי מה אני, ואמר השפלתי היא הגבהתי, הגבהתי היא השפלתי ויק"ר פ"א־ה. ומאמרו היקר סוכה נג. כשהיה שמח בשבמחת בית השואבה (ונתגדל ביראת הרוממות) אמר אם אני כאן הלל כאן ואם אין אני כאן מי כאן (כי הטוב והרע שהאדם עושה הכל בשביל עצמו הוא עושה).

His belief in individual providence [is seen] from his precious saying in Sukkah 53a, that he saw a skull floating on the face of the water [and] said: "Because you drowned others, they drowned you; and in the end, those who drowned you will themselves be drowned" (and this saying which is brought in Avot 2:6, and all the sayings there in the name of Hillel, are by R' Hillel the grandson of R' Yehuda HaNasi, as is explained in his entry).

אמונתו בהשגחה הפרטית ממאמרו היקר סוכה נג. שראה גולגולת אחת שצפה על פני המים אמר על דאטיפת אטפוך וסוף מטפייך יטופון, (והמאמר הזה המובא באבות פ"ב מ"ו וכל המאמרים שם ע"ש הלל הוא ר' הלל נכדו של רבי כמבואר בערכו).

And the Lord blessed him with a precious wife, a God-fearing woman who rejoiced to perform charity and kindness always, as is recounted in Derekh Eretz Rabbah chapter 6: Once Hillel invited a certain man and prepared a meal for him. A poor man came and stood at Hillel's door and recounted in the bitterness of his soul that today was his wedding day and he had nothing in his hand. Hillel's wife took the entire meal that she had prepared and gave it to this poor man. Meanwhile, she was delayed in bringing [food] before the guest because she had to prepare a second time. Hillel, in the goodness of his heart, asked her: "My daughter, why have you delayed in bringing [the meal]?" She told him the whole story. He said to her: "My daughter, I too judged you favorably, that all that you did, you did only for the sake of Heaven."

וה' ברכו באשה יקרה אשה יראת ה' אשר ששה לעשות צדקה וחסד תמיד כאשר יסופר בדא"ר פ"ו שפ"א הזמין הלל אדם אחד ועשה סעודה עבורו ובא עני אחד ועמד על פתח הלל וסיפר במר נפשו כי היום הוא יום חתונתו ומאומה אין בידו, ותקח אשת הלל ותתן את כל הסעודה שהכינה להעני הזה, ובתוך כך נתאחרה מלהביא לפני האורח כי הצטרכה להכין שנית, ושאלה הלל בטוב לבו בתי מ"מ אחרת מלהביא, ותספר לו כל המעשה, א"ל בתי אף אני דנתיך לכף זכות שכל מה שעשית עשית אך לש"ש.

And he was very careful about cleanliness and about guarding his pure body, as in Vayikra Rabbah 34:3, where Hillel said to his disciples that to bathe in the bathhouse is a mitzvah. And when he would go to eat, he said: "I am going to perform an act of kindness with this guest," which is the poor soul within the body.

והיה זהיר מאוד בנקיות ובשמירת גופו הטהור כויק"ר פל"ד־ג שאמר הלל לתלמידיו שלרחוץ בבית המרחץ הוא מצוה. וכשהיה הולך לאכול אמר אנא אזל לגמול חסד עם הדין אכסניא היא נפשא עולבתא בגו גופא.

And he always strove that no stumbling block should come to any person through him, as in Pesaḥim 66b: "No man ever committed sacrilege with his burnt-offering in all his days; rather, he would bring it unconsecrated to the Temple courtyard and consecrate it [there]."

והתשדל תמיד שלא יבוא מכשול לשום אדם ע"י כפסחים סו: שלא מעל אדם בעולתו מימיו אלא היה מביאה חולין לעזרה ומקדישה.

And Hillel the Nasi, when he rose to greatness and saw with his own eyes the magnitude of the destruction of the people, such that the Torah was almost forgotten from Israel because the wars had consumed the choicest of the nation, and the Idumean warrior [Herod] had wrought destruction and devastation upon the rest of the nation as well—he saw and understood that he had been sent from on high to heal the wounded. And the beginning of his work was to establish a study hall for Torah. And against this neither Herod nor the Roman rulers protested, for they recognized that his entire aim was only for peace and that he would not meddle with dissidents; therefore he [Herod] showed him favor in this and did not make his yoke heavy upon Hillel's disciples, and he also showed favor to Shammai and his study hall. And Hillel fulfilled in himself what he said in Berakhot 63a: "At a time when people gather in, scatter." And many disciples gathered to him to hear the words of the living God from the mouth of their holy master. And they said in Sukkah 28a: "Hillel the Elder had eighty disciples: thirty of them were worthy that the Divine Presence should rest upon them as upon Moses our Teacher, and thirty of them were worthy that the sun should stand still for them as for Joshua son of Nun; twenty were middling. The greatest of them all was Jonathan ben Uzziel; the least of them all was R' Yoḥanan ben Zakkai." And without doubt he had several hundred disciples, and only the choicest among them, who were the great men of the generation, numbered eighty.

והלל הנשיא כשעלה לגדולה וראה בעיניו את גודל חורבן העם אשר כמעט שנשתכחה תורה מישראל יען כי המלחמות אכלו את בחירי האומה, והגבור האדומי עשה כלה ונחרצה גם על יתר האומה, ראה והבין כי שולח ממרום לרפא הנפצעים, וראשית מלאכתו היה להקים בית מדרש לתורה, ואשר זאת לא מיחה לא הורדוס ולא מושלי הרומאים שהכירו שאך כל מגמתו לשלום ובין שונים בל יתערב, לכן נשא פניו לזה ולא הכביד עולו על תלמידי הלל וגם נשא פנים לשמאי ובית מדרשו. וקיים הלל בעצמו מה שאמר ברכות סג. בשעת המכניסין פזר. ונקבצו אליו תלמידים רבים לשמוע דברי אלקים חיים מפי רבם הקדוש, ואמרו סוכה כח. שמונים תלמידים היו לו להלל הזקן שלשים מהן ראויים שתשרה שכינה עליהם כמשה רבינו, ושלשים מהם רואיים שעמוד להם חמה כיהושע בן נון, עשרים בינונים. גדול שבכולן יונתן בן עוזיאל קטן שבכולן ר' יוחנן בן זכאי. ובל"ס היו לו כמה מאות תלמידים, ואך המובחרים שבהם שהיו גדולי הדור היו שמונים.

And as Nasi of the people, he enacted great ordinances for the benefit of the entire people, as in Shevi'it 10:3 and Gittin 4:3: When he saw that the people refrained from lending to one another before the Sabbatical year because they feared lest the Sabbatical year pass and cancel the debt, and they were transgressing what is written in the Law of the Torah, which said: "Beware lest there be a base thought in your heart, saying: 'The seventh year approaches'" [Deuteronomy 15:9]—therefore he enacted the prozbul, whereby the lender comes to the court and hands over his debts to them, and then they are considered as if collected before the Sabbatical year; and by this he saved the people from transgressing the negative commandment.

וכנשיא העם תיקן תקנות גדולות לטובת העם כולו כשביעית פ"י ה"ג וגיטין פ"ד מ"ג שראה שנמנעו העם מלהלוות קודם שביעית כי יראו פן תעבור שביעית וישמט ויעברו על דין תורה שאמרה השמר לך פן יהיה עם לבבך בליעל לאמר קרבה שנת השבע, ולכן תיקן פרוזבול שהמלוה יבוא לב"ד וימסור חובותיו להם ואז המה כגבוי דמי קודם שביעית, ובזה הציל את העם מלעבור על הלאו.

And the scholar R' Isaac Hirsch Weiss in Part 1, page 179, says that with the enactment of the prozbul Hillel uprooted a law of the Torah for the need of his time; and this is a complete error, as we have explained. And apart from this, is it not well known that during the Second Temple they did not observe the Jubilee, because all twelve tribes were not in Eretz Yisrael, and therefore the law of the release of land was not in force by Torah law; and at a time when you do not release land, you do not release monetary debts [by Torah law]. If so, he did not in any way uproot a law of the Torah.

והחכם ראה"ו בח"א צד 179 יאמר שבתקנות פרוזבול עקר הלל ד"ת לצורך זמנו והוא טעות גמור כמו שפירשנו. ובר מן דין הלא ידוע שבבית שני לא נהגו יובל לפי שלא היו כל הי"ב שבטים בא"י ולכן לא היה דין שמיטת קרקעות מדאורייתא ובזמן שאי אתה משמט קרקעות אי אתה משמט כספים, א"כ לא עקר בשום אופן דין תורה.

And similarly he enacted a great ordinance, as is explicitly stated in Arakhin 9:4: Until his days, the buyer of a house in a walled city used to hide on the last day of the twelfth month so that the seller would not find him to return his money to him [and redeem the house]; and Hillel enacted that the seller should deposit his money in the [Temple] chamber. And from Shevi'it 10:3, where they said, "This is one of the things that Hillel enacted," it is implied that he enacted many ordinances.

וכן תיקן תקנה גדולה כמפורש ערכין פ"ט מ"ד שעד ימיו היה נטמן הלוקח בית בבתי ערי חומה יום האחרון של השנה כדי שלא ימצאהו המוכר להשיב לו את כספו ותיקן הלל שיהא חולש המוכר מעותיו בלשכה, ומשביעית פ"י ה"ג שאמרו זה אחד מן הדברים שהתקין הלל, משמע שתקנות הרבה תיקן.

And not only were his eyes directed to enacting ordinances in Eretz Yisrael, but also in all the lands of the dispersions of Israel he strove on their behalf in his great wisdom. And when they came and told him that the men of Alexandria used to betroth their wives, and at the time of their entry into the wedding canopy others would come and snatch them away from them, and the Sages wished to declare their children mamzerim—when he heard this, he saw and understood that the children of Israel are holy and would not commit an act of villainy in public, and if they are not prophets, they are the sons of prophets. Therefore he ordered them to bring their mother's ketubah, and he found written therein: "When you enter the wedding canopy, be my wife"; and on account of this they did not declare their children mamzerim, Bava Metzia 104a.

ולא לבד שעיניו היו צפויות לתקן תקנות בא"י אבל גם בכל ארצות פזורי ישראל השתדל למענם ברוב חכמתו. וכשבאו וספרו לו שאנשי אלכסנדריא היו מקדשין את נשותיהם ובשעת כניסתן לחופה באין אחרין וחוטפין מהן ובקשו חכמים לעשות בניהן ממזרים. כששמע זאת ראה והבין שבני ישראל קדושים הן ולא יעשו דבר נבלה בפרהסיא, ואם אינן נביאים הן בני נביאין הן, לכן צוה להביא כתובן אמן ומצא שם כתוב לכשתתכנסי לחופה הוי לי לאינתו ועי"ז לא עשו בניהן ממזרים ב"מ קד.

Of his halakhot, not many have remained to us, because he was among the receivers of the Torah, and until his days no dispute had fallen in Israel except for the matter of laying hands [on a sacrifice], and they taught the Torah as it was given from Sinai. And only in three places do we find that Hillel disputes with Shammai, as at the beginning of Eduyot: "Shammai says: For all women, their time suffices for them; and Hillel says: From examination to examination. Shammai says: From one kav for ḥallah; and Hillel says: From two kavs. Hillel says: A full hin of drawn water disqualifies the mikveh — and Shammai says: Nine kavs." And it is brought in Shabbat 15a: Rav Huna said: In three places Shammai and Hillel disputed. And in Yerushalmi Ḥagigah 2:6, [it says] that they disputed in four places, because the Yerushalmi also counts the dispute regarding the matter of laying hands on a festival, and the Bavli does not count this because it is an old dispute (and with this, the difficulties of Tosafot in Ḥagigah 16a, s.v. Yosi, are resolved). And regarding the law of one who harvests grapes for the winepress, where Shammai says "it is rendered susceptible [to impurity]" and Hillel says "it is not rendered susceptible," they said in Shabbat 15a that Hillel was silent before Shammai.

And likewise his halakhot are mentioned in Tosefta Ma'aserot Chapter 6 [3] and Eduyot Chapter 2 [4:6]: "One who transports figs — R' Yehudah said: Hillel himself used to forbid it," and it is brought in Beitzah 35a.

וכן נזכרו ההלכותיו בתוספתא מעשרות פ"ו ועדיות פ"ב, המעביר תנאים - א"ר יהודה, הלל עצמו היה אוסר, ומובא בביצה לה.

And in Torat Kohanim, Shemini, and Tazria 13, the halakhot are brought on account of which he went up to Eretz Yisrael to hear the halakhah from the mouth of Shemayah and Avtalyon. And in Bava Metzia 5:9: "'Lend me until my son comes' — and Hillel forbids it. And so Hillel used to say: A woman should not lend a loaf, etc."

ובתו"כ שמיני, תזריע יג מובא ההלכות שעליהן עלה לא"י לשמוע ההלכה מפי שו"א. וב"מ פ"ה מ"ט הלוני עד שיבא בני - והלל אוסר, וכן היה הלל אומר לא תלוה אשה ככר וכו'.

And in Pesaḥim 115a, that he used to wrap the Paschal lamb, matzah, and bitter herbs together at one time and eat them, and his colleagues dispute with him. And in Kiddushin 75a, according to the testimony of Shmuel, that the mishnah "Ten genealogical classes went up from Babylonia" was taught by Hillel. And in Ḥagigah 9b we find that Bar He He asked Hillel, [but] it seems that this is not Hillel the Elder.

ופסחים קטו. שהיה כורך פסח מצה ומרור בבת אחת ואוכלן, וחולקין עליו חביריו, ובקידושין עה. ע"פ עדות שמואל שהמשנה עשרה יוחסין עלו מבבל, שנה הלל. ובחגיגה ט: מצינו ששאל בר הי הי להלל נראה שאין זה הלל הזקן.

And as it appears, Hillel always strove to gauge the opportune time for how to restore the honor of the Sanhedrin as at first. Because from the days of Herod's father, the Sanhedrin had been divided into five places: in Jerusalem, in Jericho, in Gederah, in Ḥammath, and in Sepphoris, as brought above. And therefore we find him in Jericho, as in Sotah 48b, that once they were reclining in the upper chamber of the house of Gurya (and in the Yerushalmi there, the house of Gadya) in Jericho, [and] a heavenly voice was given forth upon it: "There is one among you who is worthy that the Divine Presence should rest upon him, except that his generation is not worthy of it." And in the Yerushalmi there, they said that a heavenly voice went forth and said: "There are two among you worthy of the Holy Spirit, and Hillel the Elder is one of them," and they set their eyes upon Shmuel the Small.

וכפי הנראה השתדל תמיד הלל לכון את השעה איך להחזיר את כבוד הסנהדרין כבראשונה. יען כי מימי אבי הורדוס נתחלקה הסנהדרין לחמש מקומות בירושלים, וביריחו, בגדרה, בחמת ובציפורי כדמובא לעיל, ולכן מצינו אותו בירחיו כסוטה מח: שפ"א היו מסובין בעליית בית גוריא (ובירושלמי שם בית גדיא) ביריחו נתנה עליה ב"ק - יש בכם אחד שראוי שתשרה שכינה עליו אלא שאין דורו ראוי לכך. ובירושלמי שם אמרו שיצתה ב"ק ואמרה יש בכם שנים ראוים לרה"ק והלל הזקן אחד מהן ונתנו עיניהם בשמואל הקטן.

And without doubt he went there to unite all the sages of Israel into one place, but the generation was not worthy of it.

ובל"ס הלך לשם לאחד כל חכמי ישראל אל מקום אחד אך הדור לא היה ראוי לכך.

And the scholar R' Yosef Derenbourg, in his precious book Massa Eretz Yisrael, page 49a, brings the opinion of the scholar Geiger, saying that Hillel strove to raise the hand of the sages over the priests, and he says that that which we find in Nega'im, the beginning of Chapter 3, that "all are fit to examine plagues, and only to say 'impure' or 'pure' must the priest say it," that this law was made by Hillel; and his proof is from Yerushalmi Pesaḥim, the beginning of Chapter 6, that for three things Hillel went up [from Babylonia], and one of them is that which he expounded: "It is written, 'he is pure' — one might think he may be dismissed and go; the Torah therefore states, 'and the priest shall pronounce him pure,' etc." Now, aside from the fact that they have no connection to one another, as far as east is from west, behold, it is known that when Hillel went up from Babylonia to learn from the mouth of Shemayah and Avtalyon, this was about forty years before he became Nasi, and consequently his words are null and void.

והחכם ר"י דערנבורג בספרו היקר משא א"י צד מט. יביא דעת החכם גייגער לאמר שהלל השתדל להרים יד חכמים על הכהנים, ואומר שמה שמצינו בנגעים רפ"ג שהכל כשרין לראות את הנגעים ואך לומר טמא או טהור צריך הכהן לומר, שהדין הזה נעשית ע"י הלל וראיתו מירושלמי פסחים רפ"ו שעל ג' דברים עלה הלל ואחד מהן מה שדרש כתיב טהור הוא יכול יפטר וילך לו ת"ל וטהרו הכהן וכו'. והנה מלבד מה שאינם שייכין זל"ז כרחוק מזרח ממערב, הנה ידוע שעלה הלל מבבל ללמוד מפי שמעיה ואבטליון היה זה כארבעים שנה טרם נעשה נשיא וממילא דבריו בטלין ומבוטלין.

And although Hillel merited to see the death of Herod, he was not consoled by this, because from the root of a serpent came forth a viper, and the wicked Archelaus was even worse than his father a thousand times over.

ואם כי זכה הלל לראות את מיתת הורדוס, אבל לא התנחם בזה יען כי משורש נחש יצא צפעוני וארכילוס הרשע היה עוד יותר רע מאביו אלף פעמים.

And Hillel merited to conduct his exalted office of Nasi for a duration of forty years, and when he passed away he was one hundred and twenty years old. And they eulogized him with the greatest praise, and said of him: "Woe for the pious one, woe for the humble one, the disciple of Ezra," as is explicit in Sotah 48b. And his son Shimon sat upon the throne of the office of Nasi in his stead.

וזכה הלל להנהיג את נשיאותו הרמה משך ארבעים שנה וכשנפטר היה ק"ך שנה והספידוהו בשבח היותר גדול ואמרו עליו הי חסיד הי עניו תלמידו של עזרא כמפורש סוטה מח: ובנו שמעון ישב על כסא הנשיאות תחתיו.

And his study hall, by the name of Beit Hillel, remained in the midst of Israel for a blessing and for praise.

ובית מדרשו בשם בית הלל נשאר בקרב ישראל לברכה ולתהלה.

Now, regarding the matter of the two study halls, Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel, and why Beit Shammai is always mentioned before Beit Hillel: this is because the study hall of Shammai was founded many years before Beit Hillel; and the brilliant scholar, the author of Dorot HaRishonim, has already elaborated on this with good taste and knowledge in Part 1, from page 274. And we, in the course of our work, will bring only briefly that all their statements and the disputes of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel amount to approximately three hundred and sixteen, and they are of three kinds: halakhot, midrashim, and preventive enactments. The number of the halakhot is two hundred and twenty-one, the midrashim twenty-nine, and the number of the preventive enactments sixty-six, as is brought in the book Dorot HaRishonim, Part 1, Chapter 19.

והנה בדבר שני בתי מדרשות ב"ש וב"ה ולמה נזכרו תמיד ב"ש קודם ב"ה זה יען שבית מדרשו של שמאי נתייסדה שנים הרבה קודם ב"ה, וכבר האריך בזה בטוטו"ד הגאון החכם בעל דורות הראשונים בח"א מן צד רעד. ואנחנו לרגל מלאכתנו נביא אך בקצרה, שכל מאמריהן ופלוגתות ב"ש וב"ה עולין לערך שלש מאות וטז והן ג' מינים, הלכות מדרשים וסייגים. מספר ההלכות הן שתי מאות וכ"א. המדרשים כט, ומספר הסייגין סו' כמו שמובא בספר דו"ר ח"א פי"ט.

And in these places Beit Hillel retracted to rule in accordance with the words of Beit Shammai: Yevamot 15:2, 3; Gittin 4:5; Eduyot 1:12, 13, 14; Kelim 9:2; Ohalot 2:2, 4.

ובמקומות האלו חזרו ב"ה להורות כדברי ב"ש יבמות פ"טו מ"ב. ג., גיטין פ"ד מ"ה. עדיות פ"א מי"ב יג יד, כלים פ"ט מ"ב, אהלות פ"ב מ"ב, ד.

And in Yerushalmi Beitzah 1:3 and Yerushalmi Sotah, the end of Chapter 4, [it is stated] that there are twenty-four matters among the leniencies of Beit Shammai and the stringencies of Beit Hillel, and in all the remaining places Beit Hillel is always toward leniency.

ובירושלמי ביצה פ"א ה"ג וסוטה ספ"ד שבכ"ד דברים מקולי ב"ש ומחומרי ב"ה ובכל יתר המקומות תמיד ב"ה לקולא.

And in every place Beit Shammai is mentioned before Beit Hillel, except in these places where Beit Hillel is mentioned before Beit Shammai: Pe'ah 6:5; Sukkah [text: Sugah] 3:9; Gittin 4:5; Eduyot 1:13, 14; Ohalot 5:3, 18:1; Niddah 5:9, 5:6; and in most of them the phrasing "in accordance with the words of Beit Hillel" is found.

ובכ"מ נזכר ב"ש קודם ב"ה זולת במקומות האלו נזכרו ב"ה קודם ב"ש, פאה פ"ו מ"ה, סוגה פ"ג מ"ט, גיטין פ"ד מ"ה, עדיות פ"א מ"יג, יד, אהלות פ"ה מ"ג, פי"ח מ"א, נדה פ"ה מ"ט, פ"ה מ"ו, וברובם איתא הלשון כדברי בית הלל.

And in Eruvin 13b they said: "For three and a half years Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disputed; these said 'the halakhah is in accordance with us,' and these said 'the halakhah is in accordance with us.' A heavenly voice went forth [in Yavneh — Yerushalmi Yevamot, the end of Chapter 1] and said: 'Both these and those are the words of the living God, and the halakhah is in accordance with Beit Hillel.' And Beit Hillel merited this because they were agreeable and forbearing, and they taught their own words and the words of Beit Shammai; and not only that, but they placed the words of Beit Shammai before their own words."

ובעירובין יג: אמרו ג' שנים ומחצה נחלקו ב"ש וב"ה הללו אומרין הלכה כמותינו והללו אומרין הלכה כמותינו יצתה ב"ק [ביבנה ירושלמי יבמות ספ"א] ואמרה אלו ואלו דברי אלקים חיים הן הלכה כב"ה, וזכו ב"ה לזה מפני שנוחין ועלובין היו ושונין דבריהם ודברי ב"ש ולא עוד אלא שמקדימין דברי ב"ש לדבריהן.

And it was decided for [all] generations that Beit Shammai in the place of Beit Hillel is no mishnah, as in Berakhot 36b, Beitzah 11b, and Yevamot 9a.
